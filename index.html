01 What I've learned

On giving feedback

Ask first, and make room

Ask permission before giving feedback, and book the time for it — never drop it in passing.

Speak in the "I" form

I observed, I noticed, in my perception — the moment I say you did, the other person defends.

Be concrete, not generic

Vague feedback — even when positive — creates suspicion rather than value.

Keep it flowing — "what else?"

When they open up, "what else? what else?" draws out the real message — used with dosage.

The inner stance carries

Most of what lands is nonverbal. I have to find something I genuinely respect before I start.

Three kinds of receiver

One owns it; one needs several rounds; one goes into denial and can't be pushed.

01 What I've learned

On trust

  • Trust is the foundation. Feedback only works if the person already sees me as credible — the base of the pyramid, the first of the seven criteria. Most relevant with the Core team, where my credibility is still forming.
  • The iceberg. Only a quarter of a person is visible. Needs, fears and mood sit under the surface — to move someone I have to understand what really drives them, not just what I see.
  • Constructive beats comfortable. Critical-but-constructive feedback is where I learn most — it tells me what people expect and how they'd do it. "Thank you — what else?" keeps it open.
01 What I've learned

On influence & stakeholders

  • Lead with the value to them, not with my team's pain. The pain opens the door — the value is what carries. This changed the cross-team meeting and the dashboard presentation.
  • Real influence is when they own the idea themselves — the biggest shift for me, given my low influence score.
  • Ask each stakeholder how they want to be informed. Christiana and Amauri differ — email, a short 1:1, a message. Map them by interest and power, per initiative.
  • Stated agreement is not commitment. Set a deadline and a proactive check, rather than waiting for something to break.
01 What I've learned

On leading change & leading people

  • A rule is adopted only if it's concrete, valued by the team, and run as a small time-boxed pilot. Small steps beat big initiatives.
  • Situational leadership. New to the context always starts with direction — however senior the person — then I step back as they learn.
  • It's about the people, not me. I'm only as good as my team is — so I deliver even hard news in "we" language, calmly and without fear.
  • Language carries weight. And / nevertheless instead of but; when instead of if; and never the words never and always.
01 What I've learned

On myself

DISC · my anchor
High steadiness
The growth edge
Low influence
  • I lead by pulling people with me, not pushing — it's who I am, and it's how I build trust. The art is sensing when a moment also needs a push.
  • I'm motivated more by constructive challenge than by praise — and I'm learning not to project that onto people who need encouragement instead.
  • My state can show before I speak — so I name it to tame it: "I hear you, I decide not to react right now," then choose my response deliberately.
02 Strengths I'm building on

What's already working

Reflected back to me over ten sessions.

Structure

More than dashboards — I bring structure to behaviour and leading by example. You said it's exactly the structure my people need.

Visual thinking is my language

When a decision needs weight, I build a picture. Dashboards are how I communicate and lead — and the product head wanted the last one taken wider.

Problem → solution

I take a failure, gather the picture from every corner, and turn it into a concrete 20-point plan across product, engineering and country managers.

The observer's read

I contribute best by observing, collecting and re-presenting clearly — and I know people shift the moment they're observed.

And the reflection itself

You said you were even prouder that I mapped honestly where the gaps still are — that the reflecting is the hard work, and I'm willing to do it.

04 My feedback to you

What helped most — and a suggestion

Your value-first reframing changed the cross-team meeting and the dashboard presentation. Learning to influence so an idea becomes theirs. And / nevertheless instead of but, when instead of if. Situational leadership — and knowing when to push, not only pull. The iceberg. And "name it to tame it" — the one I reach for most.

A suggestion — offered with permission, in the "I" form, leading with value

May I offer one thing, the way you taught me? In my perception, once or twice the lesson arrived while I was still laying out the picture — and the read shifted as more context came out. I'd value finishing the whole picture first, then drawing the lesson together — knowing it may well be intentional.

You received it with grace — and reminded me that real life hands us fragments, not the full frame, so reacting well to partial context is a skill in itself. That exchange felt like the method working in both directions.
